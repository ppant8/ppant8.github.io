This project focused on the **bio-efficacy of botanical oils** in controlling *Callosobruchus chinensis*, a common pest infesting stored pulses in Nepal. The study evaluated locally available plant-based oils as sustainable alternatives to chemical insecticides for post-harvest pest control.

### Objectives:
- Assess the insecticidal properties of various edible and non-edible oils.
- Evaluate their effects on adult beetle mortality and oviposition.
- Promote eco-friendly pest management for smallholder farmers.

### Oils Tested:
- Mustard (*Brassica juncea*)
- Neem (*Azadirachta indica*)
- Castor (*Ricinus communis*)
- Mahua (*Madhuca longifolia*)

### Methodology:
- Treated mung bean seeds were exposed to beetles under laboratory conditions.
- Parameters recorded: adult mortality, number of eggs laid, and seed damage.
- Comparative analysis was performed to identify the most effective oil.

### Key Findings:
- **Neem and castor oils** showed the highest adult beetle mortality and oviposition deterrence.
- Oils formed a thin coating on seeds, obstructing respiration and deterring egg laying.
- The use of botanical oils presents a **cost-effective and chemical-free** method for pulse preservation.

This research supports the integration of **botanical insecticides into post-harvest IPM strategies**, contributing to safer food storage practices and reduced reliance on synthetic chemicals.
